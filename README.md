# Diesel Price Study

Research project modeling U.S. freight and economic stress under high diesel prices, physical petroleum shortages, and long-duration supply shocks.

## Repository contents

- [DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md](DEEP_RESEARCH_PROMPT.md) — original full research specification.
- [RESEARCH_REPORT.md](RESEARCH_REPORT.md) — Deep Research findings and threshold analysis.
- [interactive-model.html](interactive-model.html) — standalone inline HTML/CSS/JavaScript 3D phase-surface model with custom scenario inputs, drag rotation, zoom, and duration playback.

## Main research conclusion

The study does **not** find a defensible single national diesel “collapse price.” It instead identifies a moving phase boundary driven primarily by:

**diesel price × effective fuel shortage × duration**

with major modifiers from carrier liquidity, inventory cover, household affordability, freight-cost pass-through, and adaptation.

The $12–$14/gal range becomes especially important when paired with a sustained physical product shortage. In the central research model, roughly a 20% effective shortfall lasting around 2–3 months can move $12–$14 scenarios into the modeled systemic-instability band.

## Interactive model

Open [interactive-model.html](interactive-model.html) in a modern browser. The visualization is self-contained and uses no external JavaScript libraries.

The 90-day surface is anchored to the central LESI table in the research report. Continuous points are interpolated; values outside the published anchor area are extrapolated and clearly treated as scenario outputs rather than measured facts.
